Defects found and fixed

Three were blocking the build and the styling outright. Two more surfaced once typechecking was switched on — including one that had quietly disabled most of the content management system. All are fixed and verified.

The client would not build at all was fatal

LearningOptions.tsx imported an icon named House, which does not exist in the installed lucide-react. Rollup aborted the production build. Swapped to Home.

Roughly 490 styling classes were silently doing nothing was fatal

The Tailwind config defined only navy and gold with DEFAULT/light/dark keys, but the components use numbered shades throughout — navy-900 (113 uses), gold-500 (166), navy-600 (111) and more. None of them existed, so they emitted no CSS: dark sections rendered transparent and body copy fell back to inherited colour. Both scales now run 50–900, anchored so navy-900 and gold-500 keep the exact brand values, with the mid navy shades tuned for text contrast rather than being flat tints.

Two competing Vite configs ambiguous

vite.config.js and vite.config.ts both existed with different dev ports. Vite silently resolves the .js one first. Removed the duplicate and aligned the surviving config to port 5173, which is what the server's CORS default already expects.

Seven of the eight admin CMS tabs were unreachable was silent

Found the moment typechecking was switched on. The Gallery, Resources, Partners, Educators, Events, FAQs and Locations panels were all nested inside the activeTab === 'dashboard' block, so each one required activeTab to be two different values at once. Clicking those tabs rendered nothing at all — meaning the content management the brief asks for in §36 was non-functional for everything except the dashboard itself. The JSX nesting is corrected and the dashboard's own lower sections now sit in their own guarded block.

The About page published invented statistics Credibility

It advertised "500+ Students Enrolled", "50+ Qualified Instructors", "25+ Programmes Offered" and a "98% Satisfaction Rate". No source in the brief supports any of these, and the brief is explicit that credibility is the objective and that generic invented content defeats it. Replaced with the five tagline pillars from §2, which are true by construction. Real figures can go back in the moment Axis supplies them.

Services are now genuinely separated

Section 6 of the brief is emphatic that services must not be merged into one giant programmes section. They were: nine cards on a single page, with no detail anywhere, and three separate hardcoded copies of the service list that had already drifted apart — the homepage version advertised Coding and Robotics, which appear nowhere in the brief.

  • One content source. src/content/services.ts now holds all nine services with full brief-faithful detail, and the homepage, services index and navigation all read from it. The drift cannot recur.
  • Nine detail pages at /services/:slug, each with its own copy, section structure, SEO title and description, and conversion path.
  • The curriculum explainer from §6.1 — CBC, Montessori, Cambridge, IGCSE, A Levels and IB in parent-friendly language, closing on the "not sure which curriculum?" message the brief calls a major part of Axis's identity.
  • Special needs as a standalone service per §7, covering all 18 named areas, framed around understanding and supporting the learner — with an explicit notice that Axis provides educational support and does not diagnose, which the brief specifically asks for.
  • African languages given equal weight to foreign languages per §8, all 15 named languages present.
  • Partner-delivered activities flagged in Games & Sports, as §9 requires.
  • A services dropdown in the navigation so each service is reachable in its own right, with outside-click and Escape handling.

Verified: the production build passes with typechecking enabled, and the dev server returns 200 on every new route with modules transforming correctly.

Things the brief has not accounted for

Section 43 asks directly for anything overlooked that matters for a serious, scalable education platform. These are the ones I would raise before any further build.

A client-rendered app fought the SEO ambitions Resolved

Section 34 wants ranking on seventeen named search terms, and §33 names Facebook, Instagram, TikTok and WhatsApp as the traffic source. As a single-page app the HTML served to any scraper was an empty <div id="root">, so those link previews had nothing to read. Resolved by prerendering: the build now renders all 26 public routes to real HTML files, each with its own title, description, canonical and structured data. The stack is unchanged and the app hydrates over the markup — and only when the markup belongs to the route being loaded, so the admin and portal fallback still mounts cleanly. The remaining limit is that API-driven pages (Team, Events, Locations, Resources, Gallery) prerender their loading state rather than live content: their metadata is correct, but a content publish needs a rebuild to reach the static HTML.

Booked consultations were bypassing the pipeline Resolved

"Book a Consultation" pointed at two different places, and the one most pages used wrote to the message inbox rather than the enquiry pipeline — so the Consultation Booked stage would have stayed permanently empty at exactly the step worth measuring. All six now reach a short /consultation form that enters the pipeline tagged as a consultation.

Kenyan data protection registration Legal

Handling children's personal data at this scale almost certainly makes Guraxis Limited a data controller under the Data Protection Act 2019, with registration obligations to the Office of the Data Protection Commissioner. The brief covers the technical controls thoroughly but not the registration, the retention schedule, or who holds the data protection role internally.

Educator vetting is a promise the site will implicitly make Safeguarding

The FAQ answers "how are educators selected?" and educators travel to children's homes. There is no vetting workflow anywhere: certificate of good conduct, TSC number where applicable, reference checks, and a record of when each was last verified. Parents will ask, and the answer needs to be a system, not a claim.

Home-based delivery needs its own safeguarding model Safeguarding

An adult alone with a child in a private home is the highest-risk part of the service and the brief does not address it. Session logging, check-in and check-out, a stated adult-presence policy, and a route for a parent or learner to raise a concern all belong in the platform.

WhatsApp Business API has a lead time Scheduling

WhatsApp Business API needs Meta business verification and template approval — weeks of calendar time that no amount of development speed shortens. Start that application before the code needs it. The same applies to M-Pesa Daraja whenever payments are picked up: go-live needs a paybill or till and Safaricom approval, so the application should start well ahead of the build, not alongside it.

Accessibility is a stated brand value with no plan behind it Quality

"Inclusive" is one of the ten brand attributes and Axis serves learners with visual impairment, hearing impairment and dyslexia. That makes WCAG conformance a credibility issue specific to this organisation rather than generic good practice — including for the parent portal, since some parents will have the same needs.

Nothing was typechecked Resolved

The project was written in TypeScript but TypeScript was never installed, so Vite stripped the annotations without verifying them. Installing it surfaced 16 real errors — including the dead admin tabs above — all now fixed, with tsc --noEmit running on every build.

No pricing model behind the quotation step Commercial

The funnel in §26 has a quotation stage between recommendation and enrolment, but no fee structure is defined anywhere. This matters even with payments deferred: whether fees vary by curriculum, delivery model, travel distance or educator seniority determines what a quotation has to capture, and the pipeline now has a Proposal Sent stage with nothing behind it. Worth settling before it is needed, because it also shapes any future invoicing.

Backups have no target Operations

The brief lists backups as a requirement without saying how much data loss is acceptable or how fast recovery must be. Those two numbers determine hosting choices, so they are worth agreeing before hosting is committed.

Ownership has to be set up on day one Commercial

Section 39 asks that Guraxis retain ownership of everything. The clean way is for Axis to create the domain registrar, hosting, repository, analytics and Search Console accounts itself and add the developer as a collaborator. Transferring accounts later is the part that reliably goes wrong.

What I need from Axis to keep moving

Phase 2 can proceed without any of this. Phases 3 onward increasingly cannot — and the brief's own insistence on showing rather than telling is impossible to satisfy with placeholder content.

  • Logo files and brand colours. The mark is currently a styled letter "A" I would not want to ship. If the supplied brand colours differ from the navy and gold in the code, that is a one-file change now and a large one later.
  • Real photographs. The brief rejects a stock-photo site, and I agree — but the hero is running on Unsplash until real images arrive. This is the single highest-impact thing Axis can hand over.
  • Team profiles with the nine fields §18 lists, so the educator network stops being seed data.
  • Confirmed contact details. A phone number is hardcoded in several components and needs verifying, along with official email addresses and the real social media account URLs.
  • Genuine testimonials with documented permission, and partner names and logos with authorisation to display them.
  • Location details for each centre, and confirmation of which of the five facility types each one is — that distinction matters legally as well as editorially.
